What the Floor is

One link, visible only to the people seated on it.

Indiscipline's current site is the Bandzoogle page at indisciplinetributeband.us.com. The remake lives here as three concepts (Discipline, The Court, Red) and a preview of the editing dashboard the band would use to keep the site current. The Floor is the private place where the band and Gas-Lighting LLC seat their people, confirm their own roles, agree the terms, and then talk about that work, instead of long email threads.

Each person signs in with a code sent to their own address, sees the seat and role they were given, and confirms it themselves. Once one lead from each side accepts the same version of the terms, the floor opens. Every change, confirmation and acceptance is hashed onto the one before, so nothing can be quietly changed later. The floor itself carries no charge.

As of October 11, 2026 the floor is built and tested (31 floor checks and 19 sign-in checks pass) and is waiting on deployment. The address below is the one it answers at once deployed. Each seated person then gets a short message with the link and their seat.

Signing in

No password, no account to create. The seats are the allow-list.

https://floor-indiscipline.musha1140.workers.dev/

  1. Open the link and type the email address that holds your seat. Only an address the host lead seated ever receives a code. The floor answers every request with the same message, so an outsider learns nothing.
  2. Press Email me a sign-in code. A six-digit code arrives from floor@gas-lighting.com. It works once and expires in ten minutes.
  3. Type the code and press Sign in. You stay signed in on that browser for twelve hours; after that, ask for a new code.

Limits that protect the band: five codes per address and ten attempts per address in fifteen minutes. If you hit one, wait a quarter of an hour. Nothing on the floor is public and search engines are told not to index it.

What you see after signing in

Six cards, top to bottom.

  • Your seat, the card with the red border: your name, side, role and the address you signed in with. If it is right, press Yes, this is me and my role. That is your opt-in. If it is wrong, press Something is wrong and say what; a lead fixes it and you are asked again.
  • The two benches, each seat tagged with its role and whether it has confirmed the current version.
  • Terms, below. Only leads edit them, and any edit starts a new version that everyone confirms again. The open version stays in force until both leads accept the new one.
  • Acceptance: a lead from each side accepts the same version. When both have, the status pill in the header reads open · v1 and the conversation appears.
  • Conversation: everyone seated reads it; leads and members write; a lead's messages are tagged speaks for Indiscipline or speaks for Gas-Lighting LLC.
  • Floor record: every change, confirmation and acceptance, each hashed onto the one before. Proof re-derives the whole chain and compares the open floor to what both leads accepted; Export downloads it.

How we work with you

What the terms mean in practice.

  • Billed for time, openly. Hours actually worked, at the October 3 rate, entered from signed work sessions only. Changes asked for by email are time and are billed the same way; meetings are valued at the same rate. The build table is the open record of that time.
  • The band owns the result outright: design, code, content and the domain. The site runs as a plain static site with a small service behind the editor, so it is cheap to host and nothing ties the band to Gas-Lighting LLC.
  • A domain of the band's own, registered to the band, not to Gas-Lighting LLC. The band picks the name; the registration stays in the band's hands.
  • Pace. The October 16 show is not a deadline for the site; the Bandzoogle page stays up as long as the band wants it. Dates for the remaining items are set on the floor, not invented in email.
  • Longer term. If the band wants an ongoing relationship (the editor and content pipeline, the artist arrangement, marketing), the floor is where that gets agreed. Either answer is fine; it decides what gets built, not what the site costs.

The same way, in Sanity

The floor is how we agree. The band dashboard is how the site gets kept current, and it runs on the same rules: named people, a hand-off at every step, nothing published without a person's click.

The band dashboard preview is Sanity, the editor the band would use. Jair Montalvo owns it: the content types and the publish step. Content and art reach it one row at a time from a shared upkeep list, carried by an assistant that stages and stops wherever a person has to decide. It is a hand-off procedure, not an integration: no webhook, no sync, no code.

  1. A row is addedShows, photos, flyers, videos, posts, artwork. Laurel Guido adds her own pieces, attaches the file, writes the caption and credit, and ticks Made by hand. That tick is her attestation that the piece is human-made with no generative AI anywhere in it. Nobody else ticks it for her.
  2. The row is checkedAnything missing is written on the row and the owner is told. Artwork without the tick does not move, whatever anyone says in chat.
  3. A draft lands in SanityTitle, image, credit, caption, placement and date mapped onto the deployed content type. Always a draft, never published. The row records the document id.
  4. Jair publishesHis click in Sanity. The row moves to Published and, for artwork, records the date it went live.
  5. Mail goes out as Gas-Lighting LLCWhen a row needs an announcement, a draft is written from the row's own words, the piece credited by name, and Sepas Siddiqui sends it. The assistant never sends.
Who does what: Laurel attests her art. Jair owns Sanity and publishing. Sepas owns mail in Gas-Lighting LLC's name and settles art with the artist. An assistant carries rows between them and never decides credit, pricing or wording the row does not already contain, never publishes, never sends, never settles.
Where this stands: as of October 11, 2026 no Indiscipline content type is deployed in Sanity yet, so rows stop at Ready for CMS with that reason written down until Jair deploys one. The dashboard preview shows the shape; the content types come next.
Why it is the same way: on the floor, a seat is confirmed by the person who holds it and a version is accepted by a lead from each side. In Sanity, a piece is attested by the artist who made it and published by the person who owns the editor. In both, the record says who did what and when, and nothing moves without that person.

The art standard, tested

"No generative AI in delivered art" is a promise we check, not a line in the terms. From Gas-Lighting LLC's working notes, October 11, 2026.

Three images went through the same forensic methods: one of Laurel Guido's real pieces (Nebula 35, as a screenshot of her gallery page), an AI copy of it (a small GAN trained on crops of that piece for thirteen minutes), and a pure iterated Mandelbrot render with no network behind it. The detector is never told which is which; every image gets the same model, weights, preprocessing and threshold.

The learned detector is NPR (CVPR 2024, the line that runs from CNNDetection, CVPR 2020), checked first on the paper's own examples: a real photo scores 0.00, an AI image 1.00. Beside it, four classical reads: error level analysis (compression history), local noise consistency (capture noise uniformity), the period-2 spectral peak (a camera's demosaic trace or a generator's transposed-convolution grid), and EXIF.

The gate this feeds: an art row cannot become due for payment unless the upkeep row has Made by hand ticked by Laurel and a date it was used. That is a house rule on both of Gas-Lighting LLC's floors.

What the numbers do and do not say.

  • With the same handicap on both sides the real piece still reads real and the copy still reads AI. Resampling softens the generator's grid but does not erase it; shrinking hurts it most.
  • One generator against one piece: this shows the ordering holds, not a general error rate. The copy is a crude generator whose grid is visible by eye, so old and new methods catch it easily. A modern diffusion copy is a harder test, and both detector lines were built on GAN images.
  • The Mandelbrot render, which no model made, is called AI by the learned detector. What it measures is "not a camera capture of a physical surface", not "made by a model". That is why the artist's own attestation is the gate and the forensics are a check beside it.
  • The real sample was a resampled screenshot because the original file on the gallery's CDN could not be downloaded from the workspace, so the camera's own sensor trace was already gone from it.

What stays out of sight

The labor clock (Gas-Lighting LLC's timer, board, invoice and changelog) runs separately and is a backend for the host team. The band does not need it and nothing in it is reachable from the sign-in page. Once the floor is open, a client seat sees one extra button, Open the project view, which leads to a read-only summary; it is optional and nothing on the floor depends on it.

Signing out

Sign out in the header clears the session on that browser. Signing out from the project view also signs you out of the floor. To get back in, ask for a new code; there is nothing to remember.
