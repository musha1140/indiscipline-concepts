The art, and how it is priced

The art for the site is made by my artist, a Chicago experimental film photographer and alcohol ink artist. The standard: art on the site is made by a person, credited by name, and nothing delivered contains generative AI. In a world of AI, the things that matter most, music and art, stay human-made.

My artist's work is hers and is not part of the build table above. Instead of a fixed price now, we propose an ongoing arrangement: her pieces are placed with the band and settled as they are used, the way a consignment works. What counts as a use and how it settles is set with you and with her. If that is more than you want to decide right now, it waits; the site does not depend on it.

Why it is priced this way

Why apart from the build
Code is labor at a rate. Art is a work with its own value and its own maker. Mixing them would price her hours like mine and her work like code.
Why a gate
Because the whole promise is that the art is human-made. No attestation, no date of use, no payment.
Why an index and not a price list
Her gallery lists no prices, and a list would go stale. A formula stays fair as pieces, terms and placements change.

How a piece is paid for

Gate
An art payment cannot come due unless the piece carries the artist's own "Made by hand" attestation and a date it was used.
Fees
The band pays the price shown and nothing on top of it. Card and bank fees are carried by Gas-Lighting LLC, never added to the band's price.
Rails
Card or bank transfer in from the band. No crypto. Until the rails are connected every row is a paper simulation and nothing is paid.
With the artist
What she is paid for a use is set with her, in writing, before any piece is placed. It is not on this page because it has not been agreed.

Fixed rates: the index

The index is not a menu of pieces and it is not read off the artist's gallery, which lists no prices. It is one formula, scaled to her work, with labor kept separate.

Rate
Base × S × D × N, fixed once the piece, the use and the term are named.
Base
$400 for one piece, on the site, for one year. The anchor from the worked examples above.
S, size to depth
The piece's size-to-depth ratio relative to the reference piece in her catalogue. A piece twice the reference scale pays twice; half pays half. The reference is set with her from her own catalogue, not assumed here.
D, duration
Years the use runs. One year is 1. A single show's flyer is the show's term, settled when the show is over.
N, amount
How many placements the band takes: one site placement is 1; each additional placement (a flyer, a post, a thumbnail) adds its own count at the small-use rate.
Labor, separate
A commissioned new piece is the artist's time at her hourly rate, agreed with her before art hours begin, and billed apart from the index. The index prices the use of a finished piece; it never prices her hours.

The band is never quoted the formula. The formula is how Gas-Lighting LLC and the artist arrive at a number; the band is quoted one fixed price per use (a site license for a year, a show commission, a small use), in writing, before the piece is placed, and that figure freezes on the row. No example rates are shown here on purpose: a rate exists only once a piece is named, its size-to-depth ratio is measured against her reference piece, the term is set and the placements are counted. The attestation gate applies to every row. Everything here reads Proposed until the artist accepts it.

The tests: how a piece is checked, and what it adds

Why test at all: "no generative AI" is a promise we check, not a line in the terms, and a checked promise is worth more to the band than a signed one. Why two tests: one on a picture known to be machine-made, to see the detector say yes; one on a real piece against a machine copy of it, to see the detector say no and yes side by side. Two tests so far. The first image we tested is a known AI-made picture run through the probe below: the noise it carries is fed into a recursive Mandelbrot calculation over and over until the decay rate shifts, and the 0 and 1 at the end mean everything between a true value and not a value. It did not even need the Mandelbrot pass: zoomed in, the detector shows the synthetic uniformity of its spacing. That is the picture the U.S. Copyright Office refused to register, the refusal upheld in Thaler v. Perlmutter.

The second test took a screenshot of one of the artist's works, two passes removed from the original (a photograph of it, then a screenshot), and compared it one to one with an AI replica of the same piece. Even with all of that in the way, the real one still reads real and the copy still reads AI. The figures are on the right.

The probe is a small page that takes a picture and reads two things from it, then turns them into a price under the proposed split. Why the math is shaped this way: the gate is binary because a piece is either attested human work or it is not paid as such; the noise value is a bonus rather than a penalty because a real piece that disturbs the set more carries more of the hand that made it, and 40% is the ceiling so the bonus can never outweigh the base.

  1. 🖼The picture. Drop in any image; it opens on a public-domain one, A Recent Entrance to Paradise, made by the DABUS system and refused copyright registration in Thaler v. Perlmutter.
  2. ∇The noise residual: what is left of the centre 256 by 256 crop after the smooth picture is taken away. A camera leaves one kind of residue; an image generator leaves another.
  3. 𝓕The spectrum of that residual. Generators that build an image by upsampling leave periodic peaks on a lattice. T is the mean excess of those peaks: the tell. The method follows GANDCTAnalysis (ICML 2020) and CNNDetection (CVPR 2020).
  4. z²+cThe residual is fed as a perturbation into an iterated Mandelbrot step. Δ is the share of escape counts the picture moves across the Full and Dive views: how much of the set the picture's own noise disturbs.
  5. 0·1The gate. If T is at or above 0.5 the piece reads as machine-made and the gate is 0: nothing is paid as human work. The 0.5 line is uncalibrated and is marked as such.
  6. $The price. A human piece earns its price plus a noise value of up to 40% of that price, scaled by Δ. At Δ = 100% the mean of the three paper uses, $546.67, becomes $765.33.
